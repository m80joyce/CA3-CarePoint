Who has this problem

Working-age outpatients in Irish public hospitals, and the hospital teams who run their clinics.

Who pays or decides: hospital management and the outpatient administration department. Who uses it every day: patients (to confirm, cancel or request a new date) and scheduling staff (to see freed slots and refill them).

Patients

  • Waiting a long time on hold to get through to staff, because of long phone queues.
  • Hospital phone lines close around the time most people finish work, so people at work cannot call to cancel or change a time.
  • Already on long waiting lists, so one missed appointment can push their care back further.
  • Missing appointments because a letter was misplaced or a reminder never arrived.

Hospital staff

  • Administration: a very high volume of calls from patients checking where they are on a waiting list, or phoning to cancel or reschedule.
  • Nurses and doctors: time wasted on empty clinic slots when they could be treating other patients.
  • Admin time spent answering phones instead of refilling cancelled slots.

The hospital: cost

  • A missed appointment is an unused clinic slot that the hospital has still paid for in staff, space and equipment.
  • HSE figures cited in the project report: 525,000+ missed outpatient appointments a year, costing over €67 million. That is roughly €128 per missed appointment on average (our calculation: €67 million divided by 525,000).
  • Late cancellations are the hardest to refill, which is why CarePoint aims to free slots early.

These are the problems this concept is designed around. They are examples drawn from the project report, not CarePoint measurements.

"I got the letter weeks ago. Then work booked me in for training that morning. I rang for 25 minutes and gave up." (Invented patient quote.)
"We have empty slots at 11:00 and a long waiting list, and nobody tells us in time." (Invented staff quote.)

What any solution must do

  • Let a patient cancel or request a new date in under a minute, on a phone, without ringing the hospital.
  • Send reminders 7 days and 24 hours before each appointment.
  • Work at any hour, not only office hours.
  • Show staff a freed slot as soon as it is released.
  • Offer freed slots to waiting-list patients.
  • Leave phone and letter contact open for patients who cannot use it.

Today, hospitals mix posted letters, phone calls and text reminders. Patients often still have to phone to cancel or change a time, so slots are lost even when the patient would have attended another day.

What CarePoint is

A proposed appointment management portal: a phone-friendly website for patients and a simple dashboard for hospital staff.

  1. Patient is reminded

    Reminders are due 7 days and 24 hours before the appointment, worked out from the appointment date.

  2. Patient confirms, cancels or requests a new date

    One tap on a phone. A cancelled slot shows as available on the staff dashboard straight away; a reschedule request alerts administration.

  3. The slot is refilled

    Staff release it to the waiting list patients. The first to book gets it, and the others are told it has been taken.

Hospitals pay an annual subscription. Patients never pay.

Large or university hospital
from €100,000 / year

150,000+ appointments, several clinics or sites. Quoted after a pilot.

These prices are assumptions for this concept, not quotes. Text-message costs and one-off setup or integration work would be extra. Pricing would be tested in a pilot.

Illustrative example: a hospital with 40,000 outpatient appointments a year and a 13% miss rate has about 5,200 misses. If CarePoint helped refill one in five, that is about 1,040 slots. At roughly €128 per missed appointment (€67 million divided by 525,000, from the HSE figures above), that is about €130,000 of clinical capacity. All of this is assumption, not a result.

Try our demo

In the Patient tab, choose a hospital and enter a sample hospital number (a hint shows ones to try), then confirm, cancel or request a reschedule. A waiting-list number shows notifications of cancelled appointments that administration has released, which the patient can book. If another patient has already booked it, the notification says so. In the Hospital tab, choose a hospital, department and clinic date to see that day's patients and their hospital numbers, and release a cancelled appointment to the waiting list patients. Changes are saved in your browser and survive a refresh.

Sample data: names, hospital numbers and appointments are invented. Hospital names are examples only; no hospital is involved.

Hospitals that use CarePoint appear here. You have a different hospital number at each one. In a real version, you would see appointments from all your hospitals in one place; this demo shows one hospital at a time.

In a real version, patients would also enter a password before seeing any appointment details, to protect their privacy. This sample demo skips that step.

Tested with someone who did not build it

What they tried: A family member who is a doctor in a public hospital tested it on both a phone and a laptop. She used the Patient tab to cancel an appointment, then the Hospital tab to send the cancelled slot to the waiting list patients. She then went back to the Patient tab and booked the slot as a waiting list patient.

What broke or confused them: Nothing broke, and she found it clear and easy to navigate. She noticed there was no "are you sure" step after pressing cancel or request a reschedule, and said a patient could cancel by mistake with one wrong tap.

What I changed: Cancelling now asks "Are you sure you would like to cancel this appointment?" with Yes and No buttons, and nothing is cancelled unless the patient chooses Yes. Requesting a reschedule shows a pop-up confirming that administration has been notified.
